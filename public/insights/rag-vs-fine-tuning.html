RAG vs. Fine-Tuning: Which LLM Approach Fits Your Enterprise Use Case?

DRAFT. Pending review by Innovsol before publication.

Two techniques come up in almost every enterprise LLM project: retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and fine-tuning. They solve different problems, and choosing the wrong one is an expensive mistake.

What each does

  • RAG gives a model access to your documents at question time. The model retrieves relevant passages and answers from them.
  • Fine-tuning changes the model's behaviour by training it further on examples: its tone, its output format, or how it handles a narrow task.

When RAG is usually the better fit

  • Your knowledge changes often (policies, product catalogues, tickets).
  • You need answers grounded in, and citing, specific source documents.
  • Different users must see different content, so access control matters.
  • You want to correct an answer by fixing a document rather than retraining.

When fine-tuning earns its cost

  • You need a consistent style or strict structured output.
  • A narrow, high-volume task where a smaller tuned model can cut latency and cost.
  • Behaviour that is hard to specify in a prompt but easy to show with examples.

Often the answer is both

A tuned model for format and tone, with RAG supplying current facts, is a common production pattern. Whatever you choose, build an evaluation set first. Without one you cannot tell whether a change helped.
