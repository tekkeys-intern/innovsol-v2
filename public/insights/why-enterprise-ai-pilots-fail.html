Why Most Enterprise AI Pilots Fail — and How the FDE Model Fixes It

DRAFT. Pending review by Innovsol before publication.

A widely quoted figure says that the large majority of enterprise AI pilots never reach production. Whatever the exact number turns out to be for your organisation, the pattern is familiar: a convincing demo, an enthusiastic steering committee, and then months of silence while the pilot waits for integration, security review or an owner.

Where pilots actually stall

  • The handoff gap. A consulting team builds a prototype outside the client's environment. Rebuilding it inside real systems is a second project nobody budgeted for.
  • No business owner. The pilot is measured on model accuracy, not on a cost, revenue or efficiency outcome someone is accountable for.
  • Data reality. The demo used a clean extract. Production data is late, messy and governed by access rules.
  • Operational readiness. Monitoring, fallbacks, human review and support were treated as "later".

What changes with embedded engineering

In a Forward Deployed Engineering model, engineers work inside your repositories, your tooling and your sprint cadence from the first week. That removes the handoff: the prototype is built where it will run, against the systems it must integrate with, under the security rules that will apply in production.

A practical checklist

  1. Name one business owner and one measurable outcome before writing code.
  2. Agree the production environment and access path in week one.
  3. Plan monitoring, evaluation and human-in-the-loop review as part of the build, not after it.
  4. Ship a thin vertical slice to real users early, then widen it.
